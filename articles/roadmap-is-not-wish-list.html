The Roadmap Is Not a Wish List

DATE: 2026-02-18RESEARCH WINDOW: 2025-02-18 to 2026-02-18Internal Advocacy

For Forward Deployed Engineers deciding what to carry back from the field — and how to make product act on it.

BOLD REFRAME
Internal advocacy is not lobbying. It is translating one painful deployment into a reusable abstraction product actually wants to build.
Share to X Share to LinkedIn

OPENING — A call that did not go as planned

The eval that broke the demo

Picture the glass room at a large bank. More than a hundred thousand employees are about to touch what you built, and your voice agent just failed the compliance check you wrote yourself. The customer loves the idea. The model does not pass.

Colin Jarvis at OpenAI describes this exact pattern from 2025: FDEs embedded with a voice customer for call-center automation created evals, found the model underperformed, then carried that data back to Research. That loop, not a feature request, improved OpenAI’s Realtime API for everyone. One painful deployment, measured well, turned into platform leverage.

This is the job after the demo. Most FDE postings sell the embed. The scarce skill is the return trip.

The product team does not need your customer story. They need a pattern they can ship without ever meeting your customer.

[BEAT 1] BEFORE / AFTER / TURN: Before, you think advocacy means escalating a ticket with customer urgency. After this loop, you see advocacy as reducing a messy deployment to an abstraction, an eval, and a cost model.

Field notes that made product move

Story A — OpenAI FDE + Voice Evals → Realtime API
As OpenAI's head of forward deployed engineering has described it in 2025 interviews, an FDE team embedded with a voice customer built evals for the customer's use case, showed exactly where the model fell short, and took that measured failure back to Research. The improvement landed in the voice model every API customer uses. What moved the roadmap was a reproducible eval, not an anecdote.
Story B — Palantir × NVIDIA × Lowe’s Ontology Twin
In 2025, Palantir and NVIDIA announced work to bring NVIDIA's accelerated computing and open models into Palantir's platform, with Lowe's building a live replica of its supply chain as an early example. The field pattern behind it — customers needing continuous re-optimization instead of weekly batch runs — is the kind of signal that turns into core platform work.
Story C — Databricks Mosaic AI eval gap
Databricks launched Mosaic AI Agent Evaluation in 2024, pairing LLM judges with a human-feedback interface and tracing, after customers kept stalling at the same point: no reliable way to evaluate their agents. A repeated field stall became a product capability.

Why wish lists die

Product hears a lot of requests. An FDE who returns from the field with “customer X wants feature Y” is easy to deprioritize. Their backlog is full. Their incentives reward platform coherence, not bespoke wins.

What cuts through is different. You bring three artifacts: a reproducible eval that fails today, a sketch of the abstraction that would make three future engagements faster, and a note on what you will not build custom to force the product gap to be visible.

Diagnostic — Will This Signal Move Roadmap?

1. Can you reproduce failure without customer data?
If yes, you have an eval. If no, you have a story. Product needs evals. Build a minimal public-ish benchmark that mirrors the customer's compliance check or a supply chain's minute-level drift.
2. Does it unblock 2+ other accounts?
Databricks moved when “flying blind” was not one customer but a class. Count similar stalls in last 90 days. Name them.
3. Have you embedded with core eng?
Ramp’s model: FDEs embed within core product engineering teams to develop expertise and drive roadmap. Spend a week in their standup. Ship a small stability fix. Trust compounds.
4. What will you refuse to hand-roll?
Palantir FDEs historically faced tension: hand-roll to win vs hold line for product. Your leverage is a clear boundary: “We will not fork this connector again; here is cost if we do.”
[BEAT 2] TURN — when you toggle all four, you shift from requester to co-owner.

What they don't tell you

  • Insight 1 — Abstractions beat asks. OpenAI’s job ladder explicitly says “design abstractions to solve customer problems, then use them to scale speed across engagements.” That line is the promotion criteria hidden in plain sight. Your field note should end with a proposed interface, not a plea.
  • Insight 2 — Failure is the currency. Lowe’s needed continuous optimization because weekly was wrong. The convincing signal was the miss rate, not the vision deck. Bring the trace that shows the gap.
  • Insight 3 — Embedding is the mechanism. Ramp and Palantir both describe FDEs embedding inside core product teams. That is how feedback becomes prioritization. No embedding, no leverage. Ask for a rotation, not a meeting.

Counter — Isn't this product's job?

Yes, and product cannot do it without you. They see aggregates. You see the one deployment where aggregates break. Your value is not breadth; it is depth with receipts. The failure mode is the opposite: FDEs who never translate, so product builds the wrong abstraction from second-hand notes. You protect them from that.

One aside, in plain terms: I once spent two weeks rebuilding a connector for a retailer because I thought speed would earn trust. It earned silence. The next time I brought the same retailer’s failure trace, a tiny benchmark, and a refusal to fork again, product carved out a sprint. Lesson stuck.

24-hour move

Open your last deployment doc. Add a section called “What product should learn” with one failing eval and one abstraction sketch. Send it to the owning engineer, not the PM. Offer a half-day pairing. That is advocacy.
